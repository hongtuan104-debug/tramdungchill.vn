Da Lat Evening Street Food — 15 Night Market Bites to Try and How to Make a Night of It

What to snack on in Da Lat in the evening? 15 night market dishes worth trying, from grilled rice paper and bánh căn to hot soy milk, plus the best order to eat them in and tips to avoid filling up too early.

Evening street food in Da Lat in a nutshell: head to the night market in the city center, where the stalls set up along both sides of the street at dusk and sell until late. The fifteen dishes most worth trying are bánh tráng nướng (grilled rice paper), bánh căn (mini rice cakes), bánh mì xíu mại (meatball soup with bread), nem nướng (grilled pork sausage), grilled skewers, grilled corn with scallion oil, snails, bánh ướt lòng gà (rice sheets with chicken giblets), bánh bèo chén (steamed rice cakes in small bowls), bánh tráng trộn (rice paper salad), hot soy milk, hot chè (sweet soup), avocado ice cream, cheese yogurt and crushed strawberries. Below are notes from someone who lives in Da Lat: what to eat first, what to save for last, and how to pace yourself so you don't fill up too soon.

Where is Da Lat Night Market, and when should you go?

The street food scene is centered around Da Lat Market — the multi-story market building right on the city's central axis. By day it's an ordinary market; at night, the slope in front of the market and the streets around it fill with tightly packed plastic tables and stools, forming the night market that everyone who comes up here stops by at least once.

The night market has no shared opening hours: each stall sets up and packs up on its own schedule, depending on the season and the weather. The most pleasant time to go is after dinner, when most stalls are out but it isn't too crowded yet; the later it gets, the thinner it becomes, as many stalls sell out early, though weekends usually run later. The snacks here are everyday food — each portion is small and much cheaper than a main meal — but prices change with the season and from vendor to vendor, so ask before you order.

15 Da Lat evening street foods worth trying

The fifteen dishes below are grouped by type, starting with hot snacks eaten right at the stall. If you have just one evening in Da Lat, try these three first: bánh tráng nướng, bánh căn and bánh mì xíu mại — they're also the easiest to find around the night market.

Piping hot, eaten right at the stall

  • 1. Bánh tráng nướng (grilled rice paper) — jokingly called "Da Lat pizza": rice paper grilled over charcoal, brushed with egg and scallion oil, and topped with dried beef and cheese. Eat it hot on the spot — once it cools, it loses its crunch.
  • 2. Bánh căn (mini rice cakes) — rice batter poured into clay molds, filled with quail egg or minced pork, and dipped in a bowl of sweet fish sauce with meatballs dropped in; they're best eaten the moment they come off the mold.
  • 3. Bánh mì xíu mại (meatball soup with bread) — not a sandwich, but a bowl of hot broth with meatballs, pork skin and scallions; the bread comes on the side for tearing and dipping.
  • 4. Nem nướng (grilled pork sausage) — charcoal-grilled pork sausage rolled in rice paper with fresh herbs and dipped in a bean-based sauce; the Da Lat style often comes with crispy fried rice paper.
  • 5. Xiên que nướng (grilled skewers) — fish balls, beef balls, sausages and chicken wings on skewers, turned over charcoal and brushed with chili satay — the handiest "eat as you walk" snack in the night market.
  • 6. Bắp nướng mỡ hành (grilled corn with scallion oil) — sticky corn brushed with scallion oil and turned over charcoal, and every stall also has honey sweet potatoes roasted in the embers. Holding a corn cob that's still too hot to touch on a chilly night is a very Da Lat feeling.

Heartier bites

  • 7. Ốc (snails of all kinds) — apple snails stuffed with meat, mud creeper snails stir-fried with coconut, roasted sweet snails — great for a group of friends sitting down to nibble and chat.
  • 8. Bánh ướt lòng gà (rice sheets with chicken giblets) — rarely found outside Da Lat: soft rice sheets tossed with chicken giblets, shredded chicken and Vietnamese coriander, doused with fish sauce. Light on the stomach yet warming.
  • 9. Bánh bèo chén (steamed rice cakes in small bowls) — rice cakes steamed in little bowls, sprinkled with shrimp floss or minced pork and topped with sweet fish sauce; a group can share a tray.
  • 10. Bánh tráng trộn (rice paper salad) — the Da Lat version is rich with scallion oil and eaten while still warm. Order a small portion; it fills you up fast.

Sweets and drinks, saved for the end of the round

  • 11. Sữa đậu nành nóng (hot soy milk) — the symbol of nights in this mountain town: a hot glass of soy milk fragrant with pandan, enjoyed with a small cream puff.
  • 12. Chè nóng (hot sweet soups) — bean chè, pomelo chè, mixed chè. Sipping a bowl of hot chè as the evening wind picks up is the gentlest way to end the night.
  • 13. Kem bơ (avocado ice cream) — creamy avocado blended smooth, topped with ice cream and shredded coconut. It sounds backwards in the cold, yet everyone tries it.
  • 14. Sữa chua phô mai (cheese yogurt) — thick, chewy yogurt with a cheese flavor, lightly rich, sold by the jar and easy to take back to your room.
  • 15. Dâu tây dầm (crushed strawberries) — strawberries mixed with condensed milk or crushed over shaved ice. At the height of strawberry season they're properly sweet and fragrant; out of season they're more sour.

A few tips so the night market doesn't wear you out

The most important tip is to treat street food as tasting, not as filling up: order small portions and share so you can visit more stalls, and ask the price before ordering, since snack stalls almost never post their prices.

  • Share small portions. A group of four can order two portions of each dish and split them — that's how you make it around all the stalls.
  • Ask the price first. Snack stalls almost never post their prices, so ask first to avoid misunderstandings.
  • Bring a jacket and tissues. After a while on a plastic stool outdoors your hands get cold, and street stalls rarely have many napkins.
  • Don't hit the night market starving. The most important tip: street snacks are for tasting, not for filling up.

A sample itinerary for an evening of eating in Da Lat

A well-paced evening usually starts with a late-afternoon BBQ before the night market: get to the restaurant around 16:30 to eat well and watch the sunset, then around 21:00 head back downtown to stroll and sample street snacks.

  • 16:30 — arrive early so there's no rush, get settled, then order.
  • 17:00–18:45 — grill and eat, watch the sunset and the train passing below (trains run within the 16:30 – 21:25 window), then wait for the greenhouses to light up.
  • After the BBQ — ride back downtown and take a walk to let it settle.
  • The night market round — open with hot grilled snacks, have something light and savory mid-round, and finish with soy milk or hot chè.
  • Before heading back — pick up a jar of cheese yogurt or a box of strawberries for your room; come a little earlier and there are more stalls to choose from.

The best part of Da Lat's evening street food is that it needs no plan: wander slowly, stop wherever you see a crowd of locals, order a little, then move on. If you want a view to remember that night too, set aside 17:00–19:00 for a BBQ overlooking the valley and leave the rest to the night market.

Frequently asked questions

What street food should you eat in Da Lat in the evening?

The piping-hot group, eaten on the spot: bánh tráng nướng (grilled rice paper), bánh căn (mini rice cakes), bánh mì xíu mại (meatball soup with bread), nem nướng (grilled pork sausage), grilled skewers and grilled corn with scallion oil. The more filling group: snails, bánh ướt lòng gà (rice sheets with chicken giblets), bánh bèo chén (steamed rice cakes in small bowls) and bánh tráng trộn (rice paper salad). Save the sweets for the end of the round: hot soy milk, hot chè (sweet soup), avocado ice cream, cheese yogurt and crushed strawberries.

What time does Da Lat Night Market open and close?

The snack stalls around Da Lat Market set up from dusk and sell until late, with no fixed shared hours — every stall is different, and it also depends on the season and the weather. The most pleasant time is after dinner, when nearly all the stalls are out but it isn't too crowded yet. The later it gets, the more stalls are selling off their last stock; weekends are usually busier and run later than weekdays.

Is street food at Da Lat Night Market expensive?

This is everyday, inexpensive food — each portion is small and much cheaper than a main meal. That said, prices vary with the season and from vendor to vendor, and they're almost never posted, so it's best to ask before you order. Carrying some small cash also makes it easier when you're hopping between stalls.

Which street snacks are popular almost exclusively in Da Lat?

Bánh ướt lòng gà (rice sheets with chicken giblets) is a true Da Lat specialty, rarely found elsewhere. Then there's egg-brushed grilled rice paper eaten hot at the stall, hot soy milk sipped in the cold night air, avocado ice cream, and crushed strawberries at the height of strawberry season.

Should I have a main meal first, or just eat at the night market?

Have a moderately filling meal around 17:00–19:00, then head to the night market to graze. If you stay hungry until you get there, you'll finish the first dish completely and have no room left for the stalls after it. Street snacks are for tasting many dishes, not for filling up on one.

Where is Trạm Dừng Chill, how late is it open, and what's on the menu?

We're at 111 Huynh Tan Phat, Xuan Truong Ward, about 7 km from central Da Lat (roughly a 20-minute ride towards Trai Mat), so you can still make it to the night market after your meal. We're open 15:00–23:00, and the menu has over 70 dishes, including grill-at-your-table BBQ, hotpot and drinks, at 95,000–300,000 VND per person, VAT included.

What should I bring for street food at Da Lat Night Market?

Bring a jacket: evenings in this mountain town cool down quickly, and after a while on a plastic stool outdoors your hands get cold. It's worth carrying some tissues and small cash, and wearing comfortable walking shoes, since the area is full of slopes. In a group, order a few portions and share them so you can taste from more stalls.
