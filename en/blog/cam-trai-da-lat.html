Camping in Da Lat 2026 — 5 Beautiful Camping Areas and Real-World Tips

When should you go camping in Da Lat, and where? 5 beautiful areas around Tuyen Lam Lake, Langbiang, Suoi Vang, Cau Dat and Trai Mat, plus what to bring and how to stay warm on cold nights.

Camping in Da Lat is best in the dry season, roughly November to April, and the five areas backpackers favor are around Tuyen Lam Lake, the foot of Langbiang Mountain, the Suoi Vang – Golden Valley area, the Cau Dat tea hills and the pine forests near Trai Mat. What they have in common: pine forest or lakeside, nights colder than you'd expect, and everywhere you should contact the managing organization first rather than pitching a tent wherever you like. This guide is written from the point of view of someone who lives in Da Lat: when to go, what to bring, what to avoid.

How is camping in Da Lat different from camping elsewhere?

The first thing that sets camping in Da Lat apart is the cold. Sitting on a plateau at about 1,500 meters, Da Lat has a mild climate all year round: the daytime sun is very pleasant, but as soon as it sets the temperature drops fast — in the dry season, the average nighttime low in town is only about 11–13°C (December to March, months 12–3, according to the climate table on the Wikipedia page linked above), and it's colder still up high and by the lakes. Many first-timers see the blazing midday sun, pack only a light jacket, and end up huddled and shivering late at night.

The second difference is the dew: thick mist settles in the early morning and anything left outside gets damp, so a moisture-proof mat matters as much as your sleeping bag. The rainy season runs roughly May to October, and rain often falls in the late afternoon and evening — right when you're setting up camp. These seasonal markers are only approximate, so still check the forecast before you go.

Five camping areas around Da Lat

To be clear up front: below are areas, not ads for any particular campsite. Each area has many operators and quality varies a lot, so choose a licensed one and ask plenty of questions before paying a deposit.

1. Tuyen Lam Lake — the easiest option for first-timers

The closest to the center on this list, just a short ride away, yet it already feels like "going into the woods": a wide lake, pines all around, and lovely mist rising off the water at dawn. Good for groups with small children or older people: if anything goes wrong, you can still get back to town easily. The trade-off is that it gets quite busy on weekends.

2. The foot of Langbiang Mountain — the coldest, but you get to chase clouds

Head towards the mountain, a fair bit farther from the center than Tuyen Lam Lake. Being higher up, nights are noticeably colder than in town, but in return early mornings give you a chance to see a sea of clouds drifting below you — worth getting up early for.

3. Suoi Vang – Golden Valley — plenty of space for big groups

This area lies north of the city, much farther out than the two spots above, so allow extra riding time; in return you get a large body of water and open grassy hills, ideal for groups of ten or more who need room for lots of tents and group games.

4. Cau Dat tea hills — sunrise over the tea fields

The farthest on the list: head towards Trai Mat and then keep going for quite a long way, so check the map before you set off. The reward is a blazing golden sunrise over row upon row of tea bushes, completely different from the pine forests. The mountain road is steep and foggy in the early morning, so if you're not a confident rider, don't ride a motorbike while it's still dark.

5. Pine forests around Trai Mat – Xuan Truong — close to town yet still quiet

East of the city, along the vintage Da Lat – Trai Mat railway. What sets this area apart is its valley of flower greenhouses: when night falls, the whole valley lights up bright white, like a band of stars fallen to earth. It isn't far from town, which makes it handy for groups who want a proper dinner before heading to camp.

What to bring for a night in a tent in Da Lat

What decides whether you sleep soundly or lie awake all night is insulation from the ground up, not the tent. A cold-rated sleeping bag plus a ground mat are the two must-haves; after that come layered clothing, thick socks and a headlamp.

  • Cold-rated sleeping bag and ground mat: it's the cold rising from the ground that keeps you awake, not the wind.
  • Wear layers: a thermal top, a sweater, and a windproof jacket on the outside. Shedding a layer is easy; being one short is miserable.
  • Thick socks, a beanie, gloves: these three small items keep you warmer than even a bulky puffer jacket.
  • Headlamp and power bank: cold weather drains phone batteries surprisingly fast.
  • Raincoat or tarp: even in the dry season, the night dew gets your things wet.
  • Shoes with good grip: hillsides carpeted with pine needles are very slippery after rain.
  • Trash bags and basic medicine: medicated oil, stomach medicine, adhesive bandages.

A few unwritten rules worth remembering

Pine forests catch fire extremely easily because of the thick layer of dry needles on the ground. Don't light a fire outside designated areas; before you sleep, put it out completely with water, not just by covering it with soil. Don't leave any litter — the spot you leave should be cleaner than when you arrived.

Don't swim or take a boat out on your own without supervision; the water here is colder and deeper than it looks. If you're camping near a farm, turn the music down late at night, because the flower growers nearby get up very early. And always let someone at home know which area you're camping in and when you'll be back.

Eat well before you head to your tent

Honestly, cooking at camp sounds romantic, but it's hard work once you try it. The wind whips around, the charcoal won't catch, and washing up in icy water makes your hands ache. Many groups go a simpler route: a filling BBQ dinner in the late afternoon, then head to camp.

If you're camping out towards Trai Mat – Xuan Truong, Tiệm Nướng Trạm Dừng Chill (111 Huynh Tan Phat, Xuan Truong Ward) is conveniently on your way: from the center, head towards Trai Mat and keep going. Right next door at number 113 is Tiệm Nướng & Chill Xóm Lèo (our sister restaurant), another BBQ spot overlooking the valley if you'd like another option. The restaurant is open 15:00–23:00, at 95,000–300,000 VND per person, VAT included, with over 70 dishes and drinks, all ordered à la carte. Three sights are worth timing your visit for: the vintage train running right below the restaurant from around 16:30 (when trains pass the restaurant), the sunset over the valley from around 16:30, and the greenhouse "sea of stars" (the lights inside the flower greenhouses down in the valley) lighting up from 18:30 — all approximate times that shift from day to day, so if you want to see all three, come early and stay a while. The house signature dish is Kobe Beef with Salted Egg Cheese at 209,000 VND; on cold nights, a 300,000 VND pot of chicken hotpot with é leaves warms up the whole group fast. Have a look at the menu first, then book a table through the form on the website, especially for big groups; regular table bookings usually need no deposit, but groups of more than 10 do, and the staff will tell you the amount when they confirm. The restaurant is about 7 km from central Da Lat, roughly a 20-minute ride towards Trai Mat, with free parking for both motorbikes and cars right on site.

A sample schedule for one night of camping

The main rule for a night of camping is to pitch your tent while it's still light and eat a proper dinner before heading back to camp. Reach your campsite around 15:00, finish setting up before 16:00, and only then think about dinner and the evening.

  • 14:00–15:00: buy dry food and water, fully charge your batteries, then ride out to your campsite.
  • 15:00–16:00: arrive, pitch your tent and lay out the ground mat while it's still light.
  • 16:15–19:30: have a proper dinner. If you're heading towards Trai Mat, stop by Trạm Dừng Chill: arrive before 16:30 and stay past 18:30 to catch the train, the sunset and the greenhouses lighting up.
  • 20:00–22:00: back at camp, sit around the designated fire area, sip hot tea and keep the volume down.
  • 5:00–6:00: get up to greet the mist and clouds — the best part of the trip.
  • 7:00–8:00: a light breakfast, pack up the tent and take your trash with you.

Camping in Da Lat doesn't require expensive gear or survival skills. Pick the right season, bring enough warm layers, respect the forest and the people who live nearby — the city takes care of the rest: pine trees, mist, and a morning when you open your tent to find the clouds lying right at your feet.

Frequently asked questions

What month is best for camping in Da Lat?

The dry season, roughly November to April, is the most pleasant time, with little rain and clear skies that make it easy to catch the clouds at dawn. The rainy season runs roughly May to October, and rain often falls in the late afternoon and evening, just when you're pitching your tent. These seasonal markers are only approximate, so check the forecast before you go. On holidays and weekends the nicest areas get very crowded, so get in touch and book early.

Is camping in Da Lat cold, and what should I bring to sleep warm?

Days are pleasantly sunny, but once the sun goes down the temperature drops very fast: in the dry season (December to March, months 12–3), the average nighttime low in town is only about 11–13°C according to Da Lat's climate table on Wikipedia, and it's colder still up high and by the lakes — so don't judge by the midday sky. Most important is a cold-rated sleeping bag plus a moisture-proof mat under the tent floor, because it's the cold rising from the ground that keeps you awake. Wear several thin layers instead of one thick puffer jacket, and don't forget thick socks, a beanie and gloves.

Can I pitch my own tent anywhere in Da Lat?

It's best not to pitch a tent wherever you like. Many pine forests, lakes and hills around Da Lat are run by their own managing bodies (forest management boards, tourist areas), so you need to contact them and ask permission first, or go through a properly licensed organizer. As for fires, never light one outside designated areas, because dry pine needles catch fire extremely quickly.

Which camping area is closest to central Da Lat?

The area around Tuyen Lam Lake is the closest to the center, just a short ride away, and suits first-timers or groups with small children. The pine forests around Trai Mat – Xuan Truong, east of the city, aren't far either; they're quieter, with the added view of the greenhouse valley lighting up in the evening.

Where should a large group go camping?

The Suoi Vang – Golden Valley area north of the city has a large body of water and open grassy hills, with room for lots of tents and group games. It's much farther than the two areas near the center, so the whole group needs to agree on a departure time and arrive while it's still light to get the tents up in time.

Should we cook at camp, or eat a big meal first and then head to camp?

If your group is small and not very experienced, eating first is much easier: the highland wind makes charcoal hard to light, washing up in cold water is miserable, and if you get hungry late at night there's nowhere to buy food. A common approach is to have dinner in town in the late afternoon and bring some dry snacks and hot water to enjoy during the night.

Camping towards Trai Mat — where can I have dinner on the way?

Tiệm Nướng Trạm Dừng Chill is at 111 Huynh Tan Phat, Xuan Truong Ward — from the center, head towards Trai Mat and keep going — right on your way if you're camping around Trai Mat – Xuan Truong or continuing up to Cau Dat. It's open 15:00–23:00, at 95,000–300,000 VND per person, VAT included, with over 70 dishes and drinks, all ordered à la carte. To see all three sights, arrive before 16:30 and stay past 18:30: the vintage train runs right below the restaurant from around 16:30, the valley sunset starts around 16:30, and the greenhouse "sea of stars" lights up from 18:30.
