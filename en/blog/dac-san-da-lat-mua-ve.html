Da Lat Specialties to Take Home as Gifts: 10 Things to Buy and How to Avoid Getting Overcharged

What should you bring home from Da Lat? 10 Da Lat specialties to take home as gifts — candied fruit, air-dried persimmons, strawberries, Cau Dat coffee, artichoke tea — plus how to choose them and keep them from spoiling on the way home.

What should you bring home from Da Lat as gifts? The ten gifts guests at our restaurant most often ask about, and the easiest to carry home, are Da Lat candied fruit, wind-dried persimmons, fresh strawberries, Cau Dat Arabica coffee, artichoke tea, macadamia nuts, highland vegetables, wine and mulberry wine, fresh or dried flowers, and woolens. What they have in common: all are tied to the highland climate and easy to pack for the journey.

This article was written by Tiệm Nướng Trạm Dừng Chill in the Xuan Truong area, from the perspective of people who work in food and drink right on the road to Trai Mat, where guests often ask us, “We're heading home soon — what should we buy?”. Prices outside the restaurant vary by season and by seller, so we don't give specific figures — we only cover how to choose and how to keep your gifts from spoiling on the way home.

Three rules to remember before you spend

From what guests often tell us, many people overpay not because they're quoted inflated prices, but because they buy at the wrong time and carry things home the wrong way. The three points below solve most of that:

  • Buy by season, not by list. Strawberries, avocados, persimmons and vegetables all have clear seasons. Whatever is in peak season is usually tastiest and more reasonably priced; for out-of-season items, ask carefully about where they come from before buying.
  • Taste first, buy in bulk later. Almost every shop lets you sample candied fruit, tea and coffee. Taste a piece before deciding to buy three boxes, not the other way around.
  • Factor in the trip home. A long road trip is very different from a flight: buy fresh produce close to when you leave the city; dry goods can be bought anytime.

10 Da Lat specialties to take home as gifts

1. Da Lat candied fruit (mứt)

The classic gift and the easiest to buy: candied strawberries, persimmons, artichoke, kiwi and cherry tomatoes, each available in both soft and dried versions. Choose boxes clearly labeled with the producer and expiry date, with natural colors rather than an unusually bright red; good soft candied fruit is usually a little sticky to the touch and smells of real fruit.

2. Air-dried persimmons (hồng treo gió)

Da Lat persimmons are peeled and hung so the cold wind slowly dries them, using a Japanese-style technique, and a batch takes quite a while depending on the weather. The finished fruit has a thin coating of natural sugar bloom on the outside and is dense and chewy inside, deeply sweet without being cloying. It's the most labor-intensive item on this list, so it usually costs more than machine-dried persimmons; prices vary by season and by seller, so ask at a few places to compare.

3. Fresh strawberries

Strawberries bought locally are more fragrant and firmer than ones that have traveled a long way. Choose fruit with green stems and firm flesh, with no bruising or weeping juice; lay them in a single layer in a hard box rather than piling them into a plastic bag. For a long trip home, switch to soft dried strawberries or strawberry syrup.

4. Cau Dat Arabica coffee

Cau Dat lies in the same easterly direction as Trai Mat (counting from central Da Lat) and is Da Lat's famous coffee-growing area. Arabica — called cà phê chè in Vietnamese — likes a cool climate and high ground, so the beans grown here have a bright acidity and a sweet finish, quite unlike lowland Robusta. Buy whole beans and ask for the roast date; only get pre-ground coffee if you'll use it up soon.

5. Artichoke tea and artichoke extract

Artichokes were brought to Da Lat by the French during the colonial era and have become a specialty closely linked to the city. There are three common forms: tea bags, dried artichoke flowers for brewing, and concentrated artichoke extract. Older family members often prefer the extract because it's quick to make.

6. Macadamia nuts

Macadamias are widely grown in the highlands of Lam Dong, and they make a compact gift that keeps well. Choose nuts with evenly cracked shells; if they rattle when you shake them, the kernels have shrunk, so skip them. At home, tip them into an airtight jar away from sunlight and damp to keep the kernels crisp and rich.

7. Highland vegetables

The most underrated gift, yet the most practical: cabbage, cauliflower, carrots, onions, tomatoes, avocados and fresh artichokes. Buy in the morning, when the produce has just arrived at the market, and choose vegetables with their roots still attached and firm leaves. A box of vegetables brought home for the grandparents often earns more thanks than an expensive gift box.

8. Wine made in Da Lat, and mulberry wine

Most wine made in Da Lat is light and easy to drink, better suited as a gift than for connoisseurs to scrutinize; mulberry wine is sweeter and more aromatic. When packing, wrap the bottles in clothes and lay them flat in the middle of your suitcase; if you're flying, check them in to be safe.

9. Fresh and dried flowers

Hydrangeas, roses, chrysanthemums, baby's breath — flowers cut in the morning, with damp newspaper wrapped around the stems, will last a short journey. If your trip home is long or you're flying, choose dried or everlasting flowers: light, no water needed, and they keep their shape far longer than fresh flowers.

10. Woolens and scarves

You can't eat them, but they're always received with a smile. Scarves, wool hats and light jackets are sold widely at the Da Lat Night Market and the stalls around Da Lat Market. Try them on in person, gently tug the seams to check for loose threads, and feel the inside to see whether it itches — those two simple checks will help you avoid buying something you won't like.

Where to buy Da Lat specialties?

Da Lat Market has the widest selection and makes it easy to compare prices across many stalls in a single loop. Da Lat Night Market is better for woolens and souvenirs than for food. Strawberries, persimmons and vegetables bought along the Trai Mat — Cau Dat road are fresher because they're close to where they're grown. General rule: ask the price before you pick anything up, and favor places with clearly posted prices.

Frequently asked questions

Which Da Lat specialties should I bring home as gifts?

Ten items that are popular as gifts and easy to carry: Da Lat candied fruit (mứt), wind-dried persimmons, fresh strawberries, Cau Dat Arabica coffee, artichoke tea, macadamia nuts, highland vegetables, wine and mulberry wine, fresh or dried flowers, and woolens. Choosing by recipient makes more sense than buying to tick off a list: older relatives appreciate vegetables and tea, while candied fruit and macadamias suit colleagues.

Where is the most sensible place to buy Da Lat specialties?

Da Lat Market has the widest range of goods and makes it easy to compare prices across many stalls in a single loop. Da Lat Night Market is better for woolens and souvenirs than for food. Strawberries, persimmons and vegetables bought along the Trai Mat – Cau Dat road are usually fresher because they're close to where they're grown.

How are wind-dried persimmons different from regular dried persimmons?

Wind-dried persimmons are peeled and then hung up so the cold wind dries them naturally instead of a machine, which leaves the flesh dense and chewy, deeply sweet, with a thin dusting of natural sugar on the outside. Machine drying is much faster, so machine-dried persimmons are drier in texture and milder in flavor. Because they take more work and lose more fruit in the process, wind-dried persimmons usually cost more than machine-dried ones; how much more depends on the seller and the season.

Will fresh strawberries spoil on a long trip home?

Strawberries bruise very easily, so buy them just before you leave Da Lat and pack them in a single layer in a hard box rather than piling them into a plastic bag. If the journey home is long or the weather is hot, switching to soft dried strawberries or strawberry syrup is a safer bet. Once home, keep them in the fridge and eat them within the first few days.

Can I take candied fruit and wine on a plane?

Dry candied fruit, tea, coffee and macadamias are dry goods, so they're usually no problem; moist candied fruit counts as a paste-like item. Liquids and pastes such as wine, syrup or artichoke extract are safest in checked baggage, because carry-on luggage has volume limits for liquids. Wrap bottles in clothing and lay them in the middle of your suitcase to avoid breakage, and double-check your airline's rules before you fly.

Should I buy whole-bean or ground coffee?

If you have a grinder at home, whole beans keep their aroma much longer. Ground coffee loses its fragrance fairly quickly, so only buy it if you'll finish it soon. Whichever you choose, ask for the roast date and store it in an airtight jar, away from sunlight and out of a damp fridge.

After gift shopping in the afternoon, where can I have dinner around Trai Mat?

The Trai Mat area lies in the same direction as the strawberry and coffee growing areas, so many people stop here for dinner after buying gifts. Tiệm Nướng Trạm Dừng Chill at 111 Huynh Tan Phat, Xuan Truong Ward is open 15:00–23:00, at about 95,000–300,000 VND per person, VAT included. Arrive around 16:30 and you'll be just in time for the vintage train to start passing the restaurant (window 16:30 – 21:25) and for the sunset settling over the valley, and from 18:30 the greenhouses light up — these times are approximate.
