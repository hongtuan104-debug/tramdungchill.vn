When Is the Best Time to Visit Da Lat? A Month-by-Month Guide

Da Lat is beautiful all year round, but the dry season (November–April) is the ideal time to sit outdoors watching the sunset, spotting trains and enjoying BBQ at Trạm Dừng Chill.

Da Lat is beautiful year-round thanks to its cool climate (monthly average temperatures of about 16–19°C), but it's at its best in the dry season from November to April: clear skies, brilliant sunsets and crisp views — ideal for an outdoor BBQ. At Tiệm Nướng Trạm Dừng Chill (4.8/5 stars, 7,344 reviews on Google Maps — as of 18/10/2026), you can also watch a vintage train run right below the restaurant.

Da Lat's climate: two seasons, one altitude of 1,500 m

Da Lat sits at an altitude of about 1,500 m above sea level, with monthly average temperatures of only about 16–19°C and daytime highs usually 21–25°C — cool even in midsummer (long-term averages from the climate table on the Wikipedia page just linked). The city has two distinct seasons:

  • Dry season (November–April, months 11–4): sunny and dry with little rain, and flowers in full bloom. Nights average about 11–14°C, coldest in January — perfect for sitting outdoors around a charcoal grill.
  • Rainy season (May–October, months 5–10): rain usually falls in the late afternoon and then stops, and mornings are still sunny. Mist drapes the pine hills, the air feels romantic, and room rates are cheaper.

The key point for outdoor BBQ lovers: because Da Lat is cool all year, you can enjoy BBQ in any season. The difference is the view — in the dry season, clear skies make the sunset and the greenhouse “sea of stars” crisp and sharp; in the rainy season, mist drifts dreamily across the valley.

Dry season (November–April, months 11–4): the golden time

This is the most beautiful season. The dry weather brings brilliant sunsets, every light of the greenhouse “sea of stars” shines clear, and the vintage railway below stands out in the afternoon sun. A few memorable snapshots of the dry season:

November — the seasoned traveler's secret

November is the start of the dry season and is often considered to have the best views of the year: cloudless skies and an average nighttime low of about 14°C — cool but not too cold for sitting outdoors. Wild sunflowers are still in bloom, and it's not yet as crowded as December.

Where to see wild sunflowers: the Trai Mat – Cau Dat – D'ran route

Coming to Da Lat in November and staying only in the city center would be a bit of a waste. Tuoi Tre newspaper (article of 7/11/2023) writes that the inner city has some large patches of wild sunflowers too, but you need to head out to areas such as Trai Mat and Cau Dat to find an “endless grand panorama of wild sunflowers”. VnExpress (article of 24/9/2022) rates the Trai Mat – Cau Dat route as “the route with the most beautiful blooms in Da Lat”, puts it about 30 km from the center, and adds that the D'ran Pass and the foot of the Da Nhim Dam are also easy places to see the flowers.

A note to help you plan: Trạm Dừng Chill is in Trai Mat — the first name on that route — about 7 km from the center. Ride out to see the flowers in the morning, and on the way back in the afternoon the same road takes you right past our door, so there's no need to loop back into town and out again. We open from 15:00, so getting here in the afternoon still leaves time to sit and watch the sunset before dark.

One thing to be honest about: wild sunflowers bloom at slightly different times each year, and even two major newspapers give different dates — Tuoi Tre puts the season “from late October to late November”, while VnExpress says “from late September to the end of October”. In short, aim for roughly October–November; nobody can pin down the exact dates. If you're timing your trip for the flowers, look at photos people have taken that month before you set off, rather than trusting old calendars.

December — a sparkling Christmas

Nights average around 13°C and get even colder into January, and Da Lat lights up for Christmas all over town. This is peak season, so book your table and room ahead. An outdoor BBQ at 14°C under a starry winter sky — a Christmas feast to remember.

January–February — the coldest nights of the year, cherry blossoms and good prices after Tết

Lunar New Year (Tết) falls in late January or in February depending on the year (Tết 2027 is on 6/2); from around the 5th–6th day of Tết, visitor numbers drop sharply and we're quiet, so you can take your pick of the best tables. In February, cherry blossoms bloom along Tran Hung Dao Street and around Xuan Huong Lake — a smart time to go: beautiful flowers, fewer crowds.

March–April — spring blooms, end of the dry season

March is a paradise of flowers (cherry blossoms, jacaranda, mimosa, bauhinia). April is the end of the dry season, and clear skies make the sunset and greenhouse views super sharp. Remember to book ahead for 8/3 (Women's Day) and the 30/4–1/5 holidays.

Rainy season (May–October, months 5–10): cheaper, quieter and dreamy

Don't write off the rainy season. Da Lat rain usually falls in the late afternoon and then stops, leaving fresh, clean air and mist drifting across the valley — a beauty all its own:

  • May: the start of the rainy season, with jacaranda in full bloom along Tran Phu and Hung Vuong streets.
  • June and August (months 6 and 8): fewer visitors, cheaper rooms, and more attentive service because places aren't overloaded.
  • July–September: thick mist, with greenhouse lights glowing through it for a “lights in the clouds” effect; in September–October the first yellow leaves appear.
  • October: the end of the rainy season but still very wet (around 19 rainy days on average), and wild sunflowers start turning the hillsides yellow.

Rainy-season tips: bring a raincoat and an umbrella; plan indoor activities for the afternoon; enjoy the outdoors in the morning and in the evening after the rain stops.

What to pack for each season

The dry season calls for warm clothes; the rainy season calls for rain gear and arriving early. From December to February, temperatures can drop to 13–15°C after 20:00, so you'll need a thick jacket and a scarf; from May to October, bring an umbrella or raincoat and come to the restaurant from 15:00 so you can sit outside while it's still dry.

  • Dry season (especially December–February, months 12–2): bring a thick jacket and a scarf, as temperatures can drop to 13–15°C after 20:00. Sitting by the charcoal grill also keeps you warm enough.
  • Rainy season (May–October, months 5–10): bring an umbrella or raincoat and arrive from 15:00 to sit outdoors while it's still dry; if the afternoon rain comes, move to the covered area, which still looks out over the valley.
  • Holidays — Tết, Christmas, 30/4, Valentine's Day: book at least a few days ahead; at peak times, book even earlier.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to visit Da Lat?

The dry season, from November to April, is the best: clear skies, little rain, brilliant sunsets and crisp views. November in particular, at the start of the dry season, is often considered to have the best views of the year, before the big crowds arrive.

Is it dull to visit Da Lat in the rainy season (May–October, months 5–10)?

Not at all. Rain usually falls in the late afternoon and then stops, and mornings are still sunny. The rainy season has fewer visitors, cheaper rooms and dreamy mist. On an evening after the rain, enjoying BBQ while the greenhouses light up through the mist is very chill.

Which months in Da Lat are the quietest and cheapest?

Around June, August and September (July is busier because of the summer holidays), plus the stretch after Lunar New Year (from around the 5th–6th day of Tết; Tết falls in late January or in February depending on the year), it's quiet and prices are good. We're not overloaded, so you get attentive service and your pick of good tables.

What time should I arrive at Trạm Dừng Chill to see both the sunset and the train?

Arrive around 16:00 to catch the trains from when they start passing the restaurant (16:30), then stay on for the sunset. See more on our Da Lat train-spotting page.

What should I wear for an evening outdoor BBQ in Da Lat?

In the dry season (especially December–February, months 12–2), bring a thick jacket and a scarf, as temperatures drop to around 13–15°C after 20:00. Sitting by the charcoal grill also keeps you warm enough. In the rainy season, bring an umbrella or raincoat as well.

Do I need to book ahead for holidays, Tết or Christmas?

Yes. At peak times such as Christmas, Tết, 30/4 and weekends we get very busy, so book ahead through the form on our website or on WhatsApp at +84 989 765 070. The 17:30–19:00 slot is usually full.

Is there car parking?

Trạm Dừng Chill has free parking for both motorbikes and cars right at the restaurant. We're at 111 Huynh Tan Phat, Xuan Truong Ward, about 7 km from central Da Lat, roughly a 20-minute drive towards Trai Mat. At weekend peak times you may need to park about 100–200m further away. We open at 15:00, so arriving early makes parking easier; Grab and taxis can drive right up to the restaurant.
